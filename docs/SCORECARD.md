# Scorecard: warn and hold thresholds on simulated payments

Generated by `ScamPolicyEvaluationTest` (seed 20261014); the test fails if this file or `ScamPolicy.DEFAULT` drifts from what the code produces.

**Simulated data only.** The proportions below are assumptions chosen to exercise the rules, not measurements of real fraud. The thresholds are a starting point; real ones need real payments labelled as scams or not, and the same rule applied to them.

## The payments

- 5,000 customers, each with their own usual payment size and up to 20 released payments in the last 90 days (10% have none).
- 50,000 genuine payments: 80% to existing payees with matching names; 20% to new payees (names: 88% match, 9% close, 3% no match), 10% of those a large one-off; 1% use references with scam words (for example ISA INVESTMENT); a few pay several new payees within the hour.
- 1,000 planted scams: impersonation (30%: safe-account and HMRC stories, mostly mismatched names, large amounts, often in bursts), investment (25%), purchase (30%: ordinary amounts, names often matching) and invoice redirection (15%: new account details for a real supplier).
- For 0.5% of all payments the name check is unavailable.

## The rule

From docs/adr/0005-thresholds-by-a-written-rule.md: hold at most 1% of genuine payments; warn or hold at most 10%; within those caps, hold the most scams, then interrupt the most scams, then interrupt the fewest genuine payments, then take the lowest thresholds.
